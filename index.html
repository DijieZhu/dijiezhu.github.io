About me

ROBOTICS · EVALUATION · RELIABLE LEARNING

Building a data flywheel
for lifelong robot learning.

I am currently a Research Assistant in the Trustworthy Autonomous Systems Laboratory (TASL) at Georgia Tech. Before that, I graduated from UCLA with an M.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering in 2026. I completed my B.Eng. through the UESTC / University of Glasgow (UofG) joint programme.

I want to build a data flywheel for lifelong robot learning. Better evaluators would guide decisions during execution: when a potential failure is detected, the robot should decide whether to continue, attempt a recovery, or ask for human help. The resulting executions, failures, and corrections would provide data to improve both the policy and the evaluator.

My focus is on making this cycle reliable: selecting useful experience, retaining earlier skills as the policy learns, and checking evaluator reliability after each update. The goal is for robots to learn from deployment, complete longer tasks more reliably, and need less human assistance over time.

SCAPE: Scenario-Conditioned Simulation-Augmented Policy Evaluation

Where will a policy perform well when target-domain testing is expensive?

SCAPE combines limited paired target and simulation evaluations with large-scale simulation rollouts to predict performance for individual scenarios. It corrects scenario-dependent simulation bias and calibrates uncertainty with conformal prediction.

Ongoing researchAction evaluation · Runtime recovery

Evaluation & recovery for long-horizon VLA policies

Detecting unreliable actions before they derail long-horizon tasks.

This project studies evaluators that assess candidate actions and compare alternatives during vision-language-action (VLA) policy execution. The goal is to use these evaluations to guide correction, replanning, and recovery so robots can complete longer tasks more reliably.

Semi-supervised learningImage recognition

Image recognition with limited labels

Using unlabeled images without reinforcing incorrect predictions.

This project explores confidence-based pseudo-label selection and adaptive training schedules for semi-supervised image recognition. The goal is to reduce the influence of unreliable pseudo-labels early in training and make better use of unlabeled data as the model improves.
